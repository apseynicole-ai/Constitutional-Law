# How to define Democracy in the 1996 Constitution Context
Democracy – as a concept – defies singular definition
However, we can still get an understanding of what democracy entails.
 Theuys Roux : ‘The core idea – that decisions affecting the members of a political community should be taken by the members themselves, or at least by elected representatives whose power to make those decisions ultimately derives from the members – is more or less settled’

### Notes:

# How to define Democracy…
Roux’s idea of democracy is linked to the notion that people should prevail and have a say in how they are governed.
However,  it is not possible to have every individual vote on every decision, so this conceptualisation of democracy highlights that modern democracy is exercised through institutionalised politics that entails electing individuals or organisations to represent their interests
Roux’s definition also lends itself to understanding that democracy, as found in the 1996 constitution, embodies different conceptions of democracy

# Representative Democracy
But in New Nation Movement v President of RSA 2020 CC
CC found the Electoral Act unconstitutional as it violated citizens' political rights (Section 19) by allowing only political parties to contest national and provincial elections, invalidating the exclusion of independent candidates.
This decision necessitated fundamental electoral reform in South Africa, moving towards a system that allows for individual candidacy rather than exclusively party-based representation.
Electoral Amendment Bill – signed 20 April 2023

### Notes:
n New Nation Movement NPC and Others v President of the Republic of South Africa and Others [2020] ZACC 11, the Constitutional Court of South Africa ruled on 11 June 2020 that the Electoral Act 73 of 1998 is unconstitutional. It violated citizens' political rights (Section 19 of the Constitution) by allowing only political parties to contest national and provincial elections, invalidating the exclusion of independent candidates

# Representative Democracy
Electoral Amendment Act 1 of 2023
Allows independent candidates (not afflicated to political parties) to contest elections for the National Assembly and provincial legislatures for the first time
Came into effect for 2024 elections
Independent candidates must prove support to qualify. Candidates need to have 1,000 signatures  in support of them.

### Notes:
To be nominated as a IC
Be a South African citizen, at least 18 years old, and registered to vote in the province where they intend to contest.
Submit a prescribed nomination form and pay an election deposit.
Sign a declaration to be bound by the Electoral Code of Conduct.
Not be disqualified under the Constitution (e.g., due to a prison sentence exceeding 12 months without the option of a fine)

# Limitations of Representative Democracy
Representative democracy is the only workable system…
Failure to regulate political parties, elite-driven democracy
Inability to directly influence elected members in between elections
Influence of those not actively involved in political parties
Has Electoral Amendment Act fixed this?
One-party dominance
Affected democratic accountability and limited democratic participation, eroding separation between political parties and the state

### Notes:
Failure to regulate political parties – the fact that there is no requirement that political parties practise some form of internal democracy ultimately has the effect of conferring a huge amount of power on leadership who may dominate party affairs and impose their preferred representatives
The fact that the citizenry cannot really effect/influence directly their representatives outside of the election cycle – leaves the electorate in the position of onlookers as elected representatives take decisions in the name of the electorate without an opportunity to oppose independently unpopular decisoons
If not involved in a political party – next to impossible to infleucne decisions that affect them – lack of opportunities to cpntirnute their views effectively
Previously, one party dominate democracy – effects democratic accountability -

# Participatory Democracy
Tries to counteract the political exclusion of citizens' opinions in decision-making
Participatory democracy = affording citizens an opportunity to have their voice heard when decisions are made that affect them
Section 1(d) “system of democratic government to ensure accountability, responsiveness and openness”
Section 59(1)(a)and 72(1)(a), and 118: NA and NCOP MUST facilitate public participation in law-making processes

### Notes:

<!-- Slide number: 24 -->
# Participatory Democracy
Doctors for Life v Speaker of the National Assembly para 105-117
South Africa has both representative and participatory democracy
Which are are mutually supportive
The participation by the public on a continuous basis provides vitality to the functioning of representative democracy
The court invalidated legislation in circumstances where there had not been sufficient opportunity for public participation on legislation that significancy affects the lives of people

### Notes:
The landmark case of Doctors for Life International v Speaker of the National Assembly (2006) established that public participation is a mandatory "requirement of manner and form" in South Africa's law-making process. The Constitutional Court ruled that if Parliament fails to take reasonable steps to facilitate public involvement, the resulting legislation can be declared invalid.

Facts The NGO Doctors for Life International challenged four health-related bills passed by Parliament in 2004/2005, arguing that the National Council of Provinces (NCOP) and provincial legislatures failed to properly involve the public

Representative vs. Participatory Democracy: The judgment clarified that South Africa’s system is not just representative (voting in elections) but also participatory (citizens having a say in specific decisions between elections)

# Constitutional Democracy
A descriptive term, a decision affecting a political community made in terms of a Constitution, and this Constitution has certain basic features and characteristics
Notions of democracy (centrality of democracy); Constitutional supremacy, centrality of foundational values ( human dignity, equality, freedom etc), culture of human rights, democratic ideas, juridical review
A purposive concept – if we adhere to these features, we will establish the society envisaged by the Constitution

# Rivonia Trial
Trialists charged under laws specifically designed to criminalize resistance to racial oppression
Demonstrates how a legal system can be weaponised by those in power against the very people it should protect
Makes a case for why constitutions must entrench rights that constrain legislative power, not just procedural rules

### Notes:

<!-- Slide number: 45 -->
# Rivonia Trial
Rather than mount a purely criminal defence, the accused chose to put apartheid itself on trial.
Mandela's famous "I am prepared to die" statement from the dock reframed the proceedings as a question of political legitimacy:
can a government that excludes the majority from political participation claim the right to prosecute them for defying its laws?
This connects directly to social contract theory and the question of what gives law its moral authority.

### Notes:
Named after subburn where mostwhere arrested

<!-- Slide number: 46 -->
# Rivonia Trial
In short, the Rivonia Trial is a foundational case study in the difference between law and justice, the role of courts in authoritarian systems, why entrenched constitutional rights matter, and how the legacy of historical abuse shapes constitutional design.
It was a political win for the fight against apartheid, but with many of the leaders now in jail, the resistance saw setbacks and stagnation for several years following, in which time the oppression of the government continued and escalated .

# 1983 Constitution
Republic of South Africa Constitution Act No 110 of 1983
Key legislative reform under Apartheid Regime – to entrench apartheid  rule and response to internal protest and international pressure
Aimed to incorporate Coloured and Indian groups, to create a broader base of support for the white minority and fracture the non-racial dissent
Direct response to ever increasing urban protest and unrest, needed to ensure white control was maintained
Referendum November 2, 1983, with a 66.3% (whites only) yes vote for change

### Notes:

<!-- Slide number: 49 -->
# 1983 Constitution
Created the Tricameral Parliament: Parliament was restructured into three separate houses
House of Assembly: For the white population group
House of Representatives: For the Coloured population group
House of Delegates: For the Indian population group
Total exclusion of Majority of Black African population – complete exclusion from any representation in the central government
Created a hierarchy among the non-white race groups (similar to the caste system in India?)

### Notes:

<!-- Slide number: 50 -->
# 1983 Constitution
Competencies were categorised as “own” or “general” affairs
Own Affairs – handled by each separate house
General affairs – handled by full parliament
The Office of the Prime Minister was abolished
Symbolic State President replaced with executive State president, created consolidating power into an office not directly elected by the public but by the electoral college dominated by white ruling party
Executive authority rested with the cabinet
