The Constitution, 1996.
• Formally enacted as: Constitution of the Republic of South Africa
Act 108 of 1996
• Is now referred to as: Constitution of the Republic of South
Africa, 1996
• Drafted by the Constitutional Assembly
• Finally certified by the Constitutional Court (Second Certification
Judgment
• Took effect: 4 February 1997 (replacing 1993 Interim Constitution)

Important aspects of the 1996 Constitution
• Basic Rights for everyone are contained in a justiciable Bill of Rights
• Universal adult suffrage
• Constitutional Supremacy and the Rule of Law
• Non-racialism and non-sexism
• Co-operative government (national, provincial and local)
• Separation of powers (Legislative, executive and judicial authority)
• State institutions supporting constitutional democracy (Chp 9 institutions
like the auditor General or the Public Protector)
• Traditional leaders
• Languages

• Philosophy and doctrine that the governmental authority is
derived from, and limited by a body of (written) fundamental
law (ie a constitution)

not their own desires

Constitutional Supremacy
• Constitutional supremacy renders the constitution judicable
o Any law or conduct can be tested against the provisions
of the constitution and must be declared unconstitutional
and invalid if they fail to comply with these provisions
o Given justiciability of Constitution – an important aspect of
constitutional supremacy is judicial review
o Judicial review enjoins court to declare any law/ conduct
inconstant with the Constitution invalid.

Transformative Constitutionalism:
The 1996 Constitution is seen as inherently
•
transformative
o Because: the central aim of the Constitution is to move
from an unjust past to a just future
o The founding values in S1 confirm the intention to break
from the past

Transformative Constitutionalism:
S1 Values:
• Human dignity and equality,
• Non-racism and non-sexism,
• Supremacy of the Constitution,
• and Universal adult suffrage
Transformation as a principle is, therefore, foundational to
constitutional interpretation

Defining Transformative Constitutionalism:
No single fixed definition of Transformative Constitutionalism
•
Most famous is from Prof Kare Klare:
•
Transformative constitutionalism:
• “Long-term project of constitutional enactment, interpretation and
enforcement committed (not in isolation, but in the historical context of
conducive political developments) to transforming [South Africa’s]
political and social institutions and power relationship in a democratic,
participatory, and egalitarian direction” (Kleyn et al page 52).
•

Defining Transformative Constitutionalism:
1) Long term – it will not happen overnight – is 30+ years enough to
address +250 years of colonial and apartheid history?
2) Interpretation and enforcement, not isolation but in context
3) Substantive equality: goes beyond formal legal rights to ensure
economic and social rights are realized, reducing structural inequality
4) Proactive role of the law: Unlike liberal traditions that see constitutions as static
shields, this approach views them as swords for social change and proactive
instruments for justice.

Defining Transformative Constitutionalism:
5) Rights Protections and Promotion: It encourages a social and
legal culture dedicated to upholding human dignity and democracy in
daily interactions.
6) Non-violent ”revolution”: It is often described as an "enterprise of
inducing large-scale social change through nonviolent political
processes grounded in law

The Concept of Transformative Constitutionalism:
Constitutional Supremacy:
• Fundemental aspect of Transformative consitutionallism
• The Constitution is the highest law
o It should facilitate and enable fundamental political,
economic, social and cultural change
o “Historical self consciousness” – redress and undoing of
the legacy of colonialism and Apartheid ( post-amble IC)
o Building a future – based on fundamental human rights,
democracy, and social justice (Preamble 1996 Constitution)

The Concept of Transformative Constitutionalism:
• Value-based concept of democracy :
o S7(1) 1996 Constitution
o “This Bill of Rights is a cornerstone of democracy in
South Africa. It enshrines the rights of all people in our
country and affirms the democratic values of human
dignity, equality and freedom.”
o Designed for social and structural change

Transformative Constitutionalism
• Soobramoney v Minister of Health 1997 (CC) para 8: "We live in a
society in which there are great disparities in wealth. Millions of people are
living in deplorable conditions and in great poverty. There is a high level of
unemployment, inadequate social security, and many do not have access
to clean water or to adequate health services. These conditions already
existed when the Constitution was adopted and a commitment to
address them, and to transform our society into one in which there will
be human dignity, freedom and equality, lies at the heart of our new
constitutional order.”
• Highlights that it is both backwards and forward looking

Transformative Constitutionalism:
CJ P Langa:
Culture of Justification: “The Constitution demands that all
decisions be capable of being substantively defended in terms of
rights and values that it enshrines. It is no longer sufficient for
judges to rely on the say-so of parliament or technical readings of
legislation as providing justifications for their decisions. Under a
transformative constitution, judges bear the ultimate
responsibility to justify their decisions not only by reference to
authority, but by reference to ideas and values.”
•
