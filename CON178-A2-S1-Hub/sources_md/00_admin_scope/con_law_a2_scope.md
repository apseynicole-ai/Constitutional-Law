Con law a2 scope:
A2 S1 Assessment Scope and Preparation Guide
Comprehensive Scope of the A2 S1 Assessment
* Inclusive Curriculum: The assessment covers the entirety of the course material. This includes every topic from the very first lecture to the very last lecture of the semester.
* No Exclusions: There are no sections or chapters left out of the examinable scope. Students are expected to be proficient in the full syllabus.
* Difficulty of Spotting: The lecturer explicitly warns that "spotting" (attempting to predict specific topics to study while ignoring others) is very difficult and discouraged because everything taught is examinable.
* Repetition of Prior Content: Topics previously covered in tutorials or the A1 assessment are still examinable. Information may be revisited in the A2 assessment, potentially appearing from a "different angle" or with a different focus.
Specific Examinable Content and Guest Lectures
* Guest Lectures: Content delivered by guest lecturers is fully examinable and should not be overlooked.
* Separation of Powers: Students must specifically review the guest lectures concerning the Separation of Powers.
* Constitutional Knowledge: The assessment will require a deep pull from knowledge of the Constitution of South Africa.
* Constitutional Rights: Students must study the various rights outlined within the Constitution.
* Case Law Requirements:
   * All cases mentioned during the course are examinable to the extent they were discussed in class.
   * Prescribed readings and cases, such as the EFF (Economic Freedom Fighters) case, are mandatory for the assessment.
   * Students are instructed to complete all prescribed readings to ensure full preparation for case-based questions.
Assessment Structure and Question Categories
* Format Consistency: The structure of the A2 is very similar to the A1 assessment.
* Question Types: The exam will consist of multiple-classifications of questions, including:
   * Multiple Choice: Testing broad knowledge and specific facts.
   * Knowledge-Based Questions: Assessing the memorization and understanding of core principles.
   * Case-Based Questions: Requiring the application of legal principles to specific sets of facts or previous court rulings.
   * Critical Engagement Questions: Testing the student's ability to analyze, critique, and provide deeper insights into the course material.
Administrative Procedures and Communication
* Inquiry Etiquette: Any questions sent via email must be polite. The lecturer noted receiving rude emails regarding the scope and reaffirmed that the scope is non-negotiable and cannot be changed.
* Correction of Marks (Technical Questions):
   * If there are technical issues regarding the calculation or adding up of marks from previous assessments, students must contact the module assistant.
   * Deadline: The final deadline for mark changes or technical inquiries is Wednesday, May 20.
* Content Consultations:
   * The lecturer is open to discussions and consultations regarding course content up until the date of the A2.
   * The Caveat for Consultations: Students must arrive with specific questions. The lecturer will not "reteach" entire concepts (e.g., a student cannot simply say they "do not understand transformative constitutionalism" without specific points of confusion).
* Exam Logistics: The exam date and time have been communicated by the central university administration.
