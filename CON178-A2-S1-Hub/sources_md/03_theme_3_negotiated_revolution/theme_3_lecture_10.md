Two stage Transition Solution
The negotiators devised a two-stage transition solution:
•
Stage 1: Unelected negotiating parties draft and adopt the IC;
an interim government and the IC would govern the country
after the first election (NB 34 principles)
•
Stage 2: A democratically elected Constitutional Assembly
drafts the Final Constitution after the election.
•

Interim Constitution

The Interim Constitution
Origins and Process
•
Negotiations began during CODESA
•
Finalised by unelected MPNF BEFORE the first democratic election
•
MFNF adopted interim Constitution late 1993
•
Became immediately binding after 27 April 1994 (democratic election)
•
It was adopted in terms of the 1983 Constitution
o

The Interim Constitution
Key Features
•
Established an interim government of national unity – both the ANC
and the NP would be represented in the Executive
•
Provided a coalition government among major political parties for a
minimum of five years (up to 1999)
•
Provided for two Deputy Presidents – one from ANC and one from NP
•
Contained a Bill of Rights protecting basic human rights

The Interim Constitution
Key Features
•
Required the Final Constitution to be certified by a newly created
Constitutional Court
•
Contained 34 Constitutional Principles and provisions to regulate the
adoption of the final constitution

Constitutional Court and the Certification
•
Because the apartheid judiciary was thoroughly discredited, the IC created an
entirely new Constitutional Court
•
The democratically elected president appointed the first head of court in
Consultation with the current chief justice
•
The president then appointed a further 4 judges in consultation with the cabinet and
the Chief Justice
•
The remaining 6 judges were appointed from a list of names forwarded by the JSC
•
At least one judge (Albie Sachs) has been a member of the ANC constitutional
committee
•
The court's certification would be final and binding – no other court could thus
pronounce on the validity of the constitutional text

Provided explicitly for the two stage transition
The IC was central to a programmed two-stage transition:
•
Stage 1: Unelected negotiating parties draft and adopt the IC; It
governs the country after the first election
•
Stage 2: A democratically elected Constitutional Assembly
drafts the Final Constitution after the election

Rules for Drafting the Final Constitution
The IC set out the rules and process for the drafting of the FC
•
Section 68(1): The National Assembly and Senate sitting jointly would form the
Constitutional Assembly
•
The Assembly had to adopt the constitutional text within two years of the first
sitting of the National Assembly
•
Required support of 2/3rds majority of all Constitutional Assembly members
•
Section 71(2): The new text would have no force or effect unless the Constitutional
Court certified it complied with the 34 Constitutional Principles held within
•
If a draft failed to get 2/3rds support but gained majority support, it would become
the constitution if backed by 60% of voters in a referendum (this option was not
used)

Constitutional Principles
These were benchmarks that the Final Constitution had to meet. Found in Schedule 4 of
IC
Key principles include:
•
Constitutional democracy based on the supremacy of the constitution protected by
an independent judiciary
•
Universal adult suffrage and regular elections
•
Separations of powers / checks and balances between branches of government
•
A Bill of Rights that is Judicable, protecting fundamental rights and civil liberties
•
One sovereign state, at national, provincial and local levels (Multi-sphere
government)

Constitutional Principles
These were benchmarks that the Final Constitution had to meet.
Key principles include:
•
Proportional representation and a common voters roll
•
Recognition of traditional leadership
•
Protection of the Constitution from amendment except through special processes
