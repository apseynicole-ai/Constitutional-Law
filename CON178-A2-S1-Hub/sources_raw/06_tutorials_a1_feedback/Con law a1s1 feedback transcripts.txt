   * Example: For a $$2$$-mark question, a student should provide a targeted, concise answer. Writing pages of information for a low-mark question is inefficient and unnecessary.
* Avoidance of "Spaghetti Answers": This term refers to the practice of identifying a broad theme and "throwing everything" known about it at the question without structure or relevance.
   * Required Skill: Students must practice critical reading to identify exactly what is being asked and provide concise, accurate responses that highlight only the relevant information.
* Objective of Legal Writing: The goal is not to prove that one "knows everything" but to demonstrate an understanding of the material through clear, structured, and applied knowledge.
Question $$1$$: The 1983 Tri-Cameral Constitution and Parliamentary Sovereignty
* Correct Answer: False.
* Primary Reasoning Options:
   * Constitutional Structure: The Tri-Cameral Constitution ($$1983$$) divided the parliament into three separate houses. This was intended to provide a "semblance of better equality," though it did not achieve true equality.
   * Naming of Houses: Marks were awarded for correctly identifying the specific houses within this system.
   * Parliamentary Sovereignty: Alternatively, the answer could be focused on the shift in sovereignty. Parliamentary sovereignty was officially done away with by the interim Constitution of $$1993$$ or the final Constitution of $$1996$$.
* Performance: Most students successfully earned marks on this question.
Question $$2$$: The 1910 Union Constitution and Pre-existing Inequality
* Core Concept: The question investigated whether the $$1910$$ Constitution created racial inequality for the first time or if it pre-dated the Union.
* Historical Context: The response required an acknowledgment of South Africa's colonial and settler history involving the Dutch, the British, and indigenous groups such as the Khoisan.
* Formalization of Inequality: The $$1910$$ Constitution did not create inequality; rather, it formalized the existing racial inequalities for the first time within a unified state framework.
* Scoring Nuance: While most people received full marks ($$2$$ marks), some received $$1.5$$ marks if their wording was too vague or lacked technical accuracy.
Question $$3$$: The Westminster System and the South Africa Act
* Correct Answer: False.
* The Westminster System Analysis: The "Westminster system" as established by the South Africa Act (the Union Constitution) did not treat all citizens equally. It was a system that favored specific demographics:
   * Gender and Wealth: It primarily favored wealthy white males.
   * Legislative Exclusion: It marginalized Black and African voices by excluding them from the legislature.
* The "Cape Franchise" Exception: There was a small, imperfect franchise for Colored voters in the Cape, which served as a clue to the lack of universal equality.
* Key Skills: Students were encouraged to look for "clues" in the terminology (e.g., "Westminster system") to guide their technical arguments.
Question $$4$$: Petty Apartheid vs. Grand Apartheid
* Correct Answer: False.
* Definition of Petty Apartheid: Day-to-day segregation that established racial hierarchies between different groups.
   * Legislation Example: The Separate Amenities Act.
* Definition of Grand Apartheid: Large-scale, structural, and territorial segregation. It involved social engineering, determining where people were allowed to live and which territories they "belonged" to.
* Scoring Criteria: Full marks were awarded based on the accuracy of the description. While mentioning legislation was helpful, marks were deducted if the legislation cited was incorrect or confused between the two types of apartheid.
Question $$5$$: Legal Status of the Freedom Charter
* Correct Answer: False.
* Legal Standing: The Freedom Charter is not a binding law.
* Authorship and Origin: It was an ideological document drafted in $$1955$$ by the "Congress of the People."
   * Common Misconception: Many students incorrectly stated it was drafted solely by the ANC. In reality, the Congress of the People was a broad collection of various activists.
* Role in Modern Law:
   * Constitutional Influence: The Charter influenced the Preamble and the Bill of Rights in the current Constitution.
   * Interpretive Tool: While sometimes described as an interpretive document, its use in court for interpretation is very limited and foundational rather than frequent.
Question $$6$$: Constitutional Supremacy and Amendments
* Correct Answer: True.
* Legal Basis: The Constitution is the supreme law of the land, as explicitly stated in Section $$1$$(c).
* Misconception Regarding Amendments: Some students argued it was not the same law because it has been amended multiple times (over $$18$$ times).
   * Refutation: Amendments do not create a "new" or "fundamentally different" document. It remains the same Constitution of $$1996$$ regardless of how many additions or changes are made to the text.
Question $$7$$: The Doctrine of Separation of Powers
* Correct Answer: True.
* Functional Purpose: The doctrine establishes checks and balances between the branches of government to prevent the centralization of authority.
* Requirements for Full Marks:
   * Name the branches of government (Executive, Legislative, and Judicial).
   * Explain the concept of decentralizing government authority.
* Performance Overview: Scores for this section typically ranged between $$1.5$$ and $$2$$ marks.

General Purpose of a Constitution
* Scope of the Question: The question asked for the "general purpose" of a constitution as a concept. It did not ask for details regarding the specific South African Constitution.
* Definition and Function: A constitution is defined as the fundamental law of a state. Its primary functions include:
   * Outlining Structures: Establishing the framework and structure of the government.
   * Defining and Limiting Power: Clearly articulating the powers of government branches and setting boundaries on those powers.
   * Guaranteeing Rights: Protecting and ensuring the fundamental rights of citizens.
   * Governing Interactions: Regulating the relationships between the state and various institutions, as well as the relationship between the state and its citizens.
* Common Errors and Mark Deductions:
   * Entity Specificity: Many students lost marks by referring specifically to the South African Constitution. Because the question asked for a general definition, focusing on a single nation's document indicated a misunderstanding of the prompt.
   * Partial Credit: Some marks were awarded to students who identified correct general principles while mistakenly framing them within the South African context, though full marks were not possible for these answers.
Classifications and Types of Constitutions
* Categorization Frames: The lecture material (Lecture $$2$$) and readings categorize constitutions based on their form, amendment process, power distribution, and legal authority.
* Mark Allocation and Depth: The question required two types (categories) to be explained for a total of $$4$$ marks. Simply listing names without explanations did not qualify for full marks. Full marks required a definition and relevant examples for each side of the classification.
Form: Codified vs. Uncodified
* Codified Constitutions:
   * Definition: A written constitution where the fundamental laws are contained within a single, formal document.
   * Examples: South Africa, USA, and France.
* Uncodified Constitutions:
   * Definition: Also referred to as an unwritten constitution. The constitutional laws are not found in one document but are spread across various legislations, customs, and common law.
   * Examples: United Kingdom (UK) and New Zealand.
Amendment Process: Rigid vs. Flexible
* Rigid Constitutions:
   * Definition: A constitution that requires special, often difficult, procedures to be amended.
   * Correction of Misconception: "Rigid" does not mean the constitution cannot be amended at all; it simply means the process is more rigorous than that of ordinary legislation.
   * Examples: USA and South Africa.
* Flexible Constitutions:
   * Definition: A constitution that can be amended through the same process used for ordinary legislation or by ordinary legislation itself.
   * Procedural Similarity: There is no special legal distinction between the process for natural law-making and constitutional amendment.
Other Categories
* Power Structures: Constitutions can be classified as Unitary or Federal based on how power is distributed geographically and institutionally.
* Application/Status: Constitutions can be classified as Supreme or Subordinate based on their legal authority relative to other laws.
Critical Feedback and Study Requirements
* Reading the Question: A significant cause of mark loss was failing to follow the prompt's instruction to provide two types from one form (e.g., explaining both Rigid and Flexible for the category of amendment processes).
* Importance of Prescribed Readings: The educator emphasized that items found in the readings but not extensively detailed in the verbal lecture were explicitly tested. These reading materials will be included in future assessments.
* Tutorial Attendance: Discrepancies in performance were noted between students who attended tutorials/classes and those who did not, particularly regarding the ability to provide examples and nuanced definitions.
