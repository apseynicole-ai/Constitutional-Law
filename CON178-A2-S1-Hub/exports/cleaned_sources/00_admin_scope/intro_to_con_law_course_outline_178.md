1. Word of welcome and prerequisite requirements
Welcome to Introduction to Constitutional Law and Statutory Interpretation 178. In this
year module we will discuss the foundations and historical development of South
African constitutional law, constitutionalism, constitutional supremacy, constitutional
values, rule of law, democracy and separation of powers. We will also discuss the
status and role of legislation as a source of law; dimensions, theories and principles
of and approaches to constitutional and statutory interpretation; and the relation
between language, context and values in constitutional and statutory interpretation.

3.3 Notional hours
Notional hours represents the agreed estimated learning time taken by the average
student to achieve the specified learning outcomes of a module (DHET, 2014; DHET,
5

2021). This is not a precise measure but indicates the amount of study time and
degree of commitment expected. This includes, inter alia, consideration of contact
time (in lectures and tutorials (if applicable)), any research, completion of
assignments, individual learning of the content and all assessments. Each credit
allocated to a module represents ten notional hours, i.e., a ten-credit module is equal
to 100 notional hours and would indicate that the average student would need 100
hours to achieve the identified outcomes and obtain the necessary knowledge and
skills.
Please note the following about the notional hours for Introduction to Constitutional
Law and Statutory Interpretation 178:
• It is a year module comprising 18 credits, which means that students should
spend 180 notional hours working on the module contents.
• The module is furthermore divided into 90 notional hours per semester.
• Both the first and second semester of 2025 is 13 weeks long.
• This division translates to roughly 6 hours per week that students should
devote to Introduction to Constitutional Law and Statutory Interpretation
178 during both semesters.
• Students should bear in mind that 2 hours per week will be dedicated to
contact lectures (including 1 hour per week for tutorials), the remaining 4
hours per week should be divided between time spent on preparing for
lectures, engaging with prescribed material, carrying out formative and
summative assessments (tests tutorials) and time spent on private study.

4. Teaching and learning approach
Introduction to Constitutional Law and Statutory Interpretation 178 consists of formal
lectures, as well as practical sessions in the form of tutorials. Prescribed reading and
cases are indicated in the respective study guides and form an essential part of the
course. They should be studied in detail.
Here are a few hints about how to approach the study material:
• In this module you are required to engage with a variety of sources and
learning materials, including the Constitution, legislation, case law and
academic literature. These sources and materials are important and need to be
studied. It is not sufficient simply to focus on the academic literature or the
class notes.
8

• The sources and materials are interrelated, and must not be studied in isolation.
It is important to be able to integrate them in a meaningful way.
• The study guides and the lectures provide important pointers as to the focus
of each theme, and should assist you in integrating the different materials. It is
nevertheless important to point out that the lectures are not meant to be
comprehensive, and that you should use your class notes together with, and
not as a substitute for, the prescribed materials.
• Students sometimes underestimate the importance of case law. Please do not
make that mistake. It is important to be able to discuss case law and to use it
to back up and substantiate your answers.
• You are required to prepare for lectures by reading the prescribed reading
material for each theme.
5. Outcomes of the module
Upon completion of this module, you should be able to:
• Discuss the social and political forces and events that gave rise to the
Constitution, 1996;
• Discuss the social, economic, political, global, and historical context within
which the Constitution, 1996, is interpreted and applied;
• Discuss, apply, and evaluate the basic constitutional concepts, values and
principles embedded in the 1996 Constitution;
• Identify and apply the rules governing the commencement and demise of
legislation, and legislation’s relationship to other sources of law;
• Compare, apply and critically evaluate different theories and dimensions of
and approaches to constitutional and statutory interpretation.

7 3 Calculation of mid-year mark
MY (mid-year) marks are calculated after A2S1.
If a student wrote both A1S1 and A2S1, the following formula will be applied to
calculate the mid-year mark (the percentage marks of AFS1, A1S1 and A2S1 are
combined according to their respective weights):
MY = (0.10/0.40 x percentage mark AFS1) + (0.15/0.40 x percentage mark A1S1) +
(0.25/0.40 x percentage mark A2S1)
If a student wrote only A1S1, the following formula will be applied to calculate the
mid-year mark (the percentage marks of AFS1 and A1S1 are combined according to
their respective weights):
MY = (0.05/0.20 x percentage mark AFS1) + (0.15/0.20 x percentage mark A1S1)
If a student wrote only A2S1, the following formula will be applied to calculate the
mid-year mark (the percentage marks of AFS1 and A2S1 are combined according to
their respective weights):
MY = (0.05/0.25 x percentage mark AFS1) + (0.20/0.25 x percentage mark A2S1)
If a student wrote neither of the A1S1 or A2S1 assessments, the mid-year mark will be
zero.

A FM is calculated after A2S2 if a student completed A1S1, A2S1, A1S2 and A2S2,

Therefore, the FM after A2S2 if a student completed A1S1, A2S1, A1S2 and A2S2 is

percentage mark AFS1 (0.05) + percentage mark AFS2 (0.10) + percentage mark A1S1

7.5. Assessment queries
In the event that students wish to discuss or query an assessment result, they are
expected to arrange a consultation via email with the lecturer not later than 1 week
after the release of the memorandum/class/tutorial discussion of the assessment.
Furthermore, the student is expected to provide written motivation for the
reassessment based on the provided memorandum in the email request for a
consultation.
No queries or adjustments of assessment results will be entertained in the absence
of the student having proof of their attempts at arranging said consultation within the
timeframe mentioned above.
