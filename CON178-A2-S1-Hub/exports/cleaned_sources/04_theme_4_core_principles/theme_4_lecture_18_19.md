Value-based constitutionalism
1. Normative
• The Constitution is based on moral values, not just legal rules
2. Interpretive
• Values guide how courts understand and apply rights
3. Transformative
• The Constitution aims to change society and address injustice
4. Contextual
• Must respond to and reflect South Africa’s social and historical context
5. Counter-majoritarian
• Courts can uphold values even against public opinion

Bill of Rights
•
Bill of Rights has been constructed in such a way to give effect
- to the transformative vision of the Constitution, 1996
- • It sets out a unique human rights vision for South Africa
• The broad range of rights is aimed at facilitating political and
- socio-economic transformation in South Africa while
- protecting the human dignity of all persons within the State
• It has broad application – both to the State and to private
parties

The Application of the Bill of Rights
When litigating the Bill of Rights, the Court
●
has to determine whether the BOR applies
Directly or Indirectly
○
Vertically or Horizontally

The Application of the Bill of Rights
● Direct application:
○ Direct application of the Bill of Rights occurs when a person, company, or state
organ violates a constitutional right found in chp 2
○ Or when the ordinary rules of law (Legislation, common law, customary law) are
not consistent with the Bill of Rights
○ If they are not, the BOR if applied directly will override these rules of law – ie law
will be declared invalid by the courts
○ Example: company firing a woman due to pregnancy (violating section 9
equality rights), would be unconstitutional and invalid
○ Example: S v Mawanyane – Death Penalty declared unconstitutional

The Application of the Bill of Rights
● Indirect application: the purpose is to determine whether the ordinary rules of the
law promote the values of the BOR
○ If they do not, the court will use the BOR to develop the rules of law (s39(2))
○ Example: the development of common law to align with constitutional values, as
seen inCarmichelev Minister of Safety and Security. (2001) CC: The Court used
s39(2) to interpret the law of delict to promote rights and constitutional values,
rather than directly invalidating a statute.
■ IE: Instead of declaring a law invalid (direct application), courts interpret
legislation or develop common law/customary law to conform with the Bill
of Rights

The application of the Bill of Rights
● The next question is then, is it a vertical or horizontal
application?
○ This refers to the different relationships and legal
situations in which the Bill of Rights is enforced

The application of the Bill of Rights
Vertical application
● Governed by s8(1) – looks at the relationship between the state and private
individuals
● Imposes obligations on the state to respect, protect and fulfil rights in the BOR
● Acts an additional check on state power – therefore binds the legislative, executive,
judiciary and all organs of state
● Protects citizens from abuse of power by the government
● Example:
○ A person brings a claim against the police for unlawful arrest, or someone
who challenges a government policy that they claim violates their rights

The application of the Bill of Rights
Examples of vertical application of rights :
● Right to Equality (Section 9): An individual sues a government department for
refusing to hire or promote them based on race, gender, or sexual orientation.
● Freedom of Expression (Section 16): A journalist or citizen challenges the police
for arresting them for participating in a lawful public protest.
● Right to Access Information (Section 32): A person uses the PAIA act to force a
public body, eg DHA, to disclose records held by the state.

The application of the Bill of Rights
● Socio-economic Rights (Sections 26 & 27): Communities challenging the
government for failing to provide adequate, basic water services or, as in
the Grootboom case, reasonable access to housing.
● Right to Fair Trial (Section 35): An accused person argues that their rights were
violated by the police or prosecutor during their arrest or detention.
● Property Rights (Section 25): A landowner challenges the state's expropriation of
their property, arguing it was not done for a public purpose or without fair
compensation.

The application of the Bill of Rights
Horizontal application
● Governed by s8(2) – requires considering the nature of the right and duty
imposed
● When applied horizontally, this imposes obligations between private persons
(individuals or companies) to respect the rights of the BOR (in certain
circumstances)
○ Protects citizens from violations of rights by private parties such as unfair
discrimination by an employer
○ IE you don’t have to rely on the common law or legislation – you can say
“this person has violated my constitutional right”

The application of the Bill of Rights
Direct Horizontal application
● Governed by s8(2): The BOR applies directly to the conduct of private
individuals, allowing them to rely directly on a constitutional right for a
claim
Indirect Horizontal Application
● S39(2): The BOR influences private law by requiring courts to interpret
legislation or develop the common law in a way that promotes the sprite,
purport, and object of the BOR

The application of the Bill of Rights
Example 1: Equality (S9)
● Scenario: A private employer refuses to hire someone based on their race,
gender, or disability.
● Even though this is a private employer (not the state) the right to equality
can apply directly
● The private individual can argue, “my constitutional right to equality has
been infringed by this private actor”
● Equality is a right that clearly makes sense in private relationships
(employment, services etc)
● Courts recognised that discrimination is not only a state problem

The application of the Bill of Rights
Example 2: Freedom of Expression (s16)
Scenario: A private media company refuses to publish an opinion article
● The private individual might try to rely on freedom of expression
● BUT the court will have to balance
○ The writers freedom of expression
○ The companies freedom to choose what it publishes
○ Rights can apply directly but can compete with other rights in
private relationships

The application of the Bill of Rights
Example 3: Right to housing (s26)
Scenario: A private land owner evicts people living on their land without following the legal
processes
●
Those evicted can rely directly on the right to access housing
●
They can argue “this eviction violates my right to housing”
●
Rights will still apply between the private individuals
●
The Court will ask
○
Is eviction just and equitable
○
What processes were followed
○
What is the impact on the vulnerable people
●
Again we see competing rights of private individuals as the land owner has his own proterty
rights protected by s25

The application of the Bill of Rights
Example 4: Right to education (s29)
Scenario: Private school refuses to admit a learner without a fair and lawful
reason (arbitrary exclusion)
● The learner or parent may rely directly on s29 (right to basic education)
● They can argue “The decision unjusrifably limites my right to education”
● Private schools are private individuals (not the state)

The application of the Bill of Rights
Example 4: Right to education (s29)
Scenario: Private school refuses to admit a learner without a fair and lawful reason
(arbitrary exclusion)
● Again, the court will ask :
○ Is exclusion reasonable
○ Does it undermine the right to basic education
○ Are there other justifications such as contractual or policy justifications
○ Nuance: The right to basic education is a very strong right (immediately
realisable)
○ But the balance will be with the school's autonomy to decide who they admit

The application of the Bill of Rights
Key take aways from the examples:
●
Rights can shape private relationships, especially where there is
○
A power imbalance
○
Basic human needs are effected
●
Direct horizontal application means you can use the constitution itself in disputes with
other private parties – but courts will always ask whether it is appropriate nd how rights
should be balanced
●
It will depend on
○
The nature of the right
○
The relationship between the parties
○
And the need to balance competing rights
