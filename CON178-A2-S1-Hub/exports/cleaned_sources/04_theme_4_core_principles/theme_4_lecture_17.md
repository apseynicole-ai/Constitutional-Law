Value based constitutionalism
• Value-based constitutionalism is a legal concept that emphasises
that constitutions are not just legal, procedural documents but
are rooted in core ethical, moral, and social values.
• It prioritises these underlying principles to guide the
interpretation of rights and laws.
• Constitutional values are the foundational principles that underpin
a democratic state's legal framework and guide its governance.

Value based constitutionalism

Value based constitutionalism
Foundational Case: S v Makwayane 1995 (3) SA 391 (CC)
• Facts:
• Makwanyane and Mchunu were convicted in the Witwatersrand Local Division on
four counts of murder and sentenced to death under section 277(1)(a) of
the Criminal Procedure Act 51 of 1977.
• Their appeal raised a constitutional challenge under the Interim Constitution of
1993, arguing that the death penalty violated the rights to life (section 9), human
dignity (section 10), and protection against cruel, inhuman, or degrading
punishment (section 11(2))
• The case was referred to the newly created Constitutional Court, marking the first
constitutional review of legislation in post-apartheid South Africa.

Value based constitutionalism
The limitations Clause
• Rights are not absolute – they can in certain circumstances, be limited
• S36 contains a limitation clause – that ALLOWS for the
constitutionally valid limitation of rights in certain circumstances
• Limitation is determined by a two stage enquiry
• First court must established a right has been infringed upon
(limited)
• Second the court must determine whether it is justifiable

Value based constitutionalism
The limitations Clause
S36 Constitution, 1996 (S33 (1) IC)
• S36: The rights in the Bill of Rights may be limited only in terms of the law of
general application, to the extent that the limitation is reasonable and justifiable
in an open and democratic society based on human dignity, equality, and
freedom
• Factors to guide whether the limitation is reasonable and justifiable
• Nature of the right
• Importance and relationship between the purpose of limitation and the
limitation
• There are no less restrictive means to achieve the purpose

Value based constitutionalism
The limitations Clause
Re: S v Walters [para 26-27] : This is essentially a two-stage exercise. First, there is the threshold
enquiry aimed at determining whether or not the enactment in question constitutes a limitation on one or
other guaranteed right. This entails examining (a) the content and scope of the relevant protected right(s)
and (b) the meaning and effect of the impugned enactment to see whether there is any limitation of (a) by
(b). Subsections (1) and (2) of section 39 of the Constitution provide guidance on the interpretation of both
the rights and the enactment, essentially requiring that they be interpreted to promote the value system
of an open and democratic society based on human dignity, equality and freedom. If upon such
analysis no limitation is found, that is the end of the matter. The constitutional challenge is dismissed there
and then.
If there is indeed a limitation, however, the second stage ensues. This is ordinarily called the limitations
exercise. In essence this requires a weighing-up of the nature and importance of the right(s) that are limited
together with the extent of the limitation as against the importance and purpose of the limiting enactment.

Value based constitutionalism
Balancing competing rights using values
• Courts balance competing rights by asking which outcome best reflects
constitutional values.
• Port Elizabeth Municipality v Various Occupiers2005 (1) SA 217 (CC))
• Competing rights – right to private property and right to housing
• Justice Sachs emphasised that fairness required considering both the
owners’ rights and the occupiers’ human dignity, especially in the
context of South Africa’s history of forced removals.

Value-based constitutionalism
Values driving social transformation
• As discussed under Transformative Constitutionalism, one of the goals of the Constitution,
1996 is to change and transform society, not just resolve disputes.
• Minister of Home Affairs v Fourie 2006 (1) SA 524 (CC)
• Right to Equality and Same-sex marriage
• Justice Sachs Denying same-sex couples access to marriage contravened rights and
values of equality and human dignity. Excluding same-sex couples from marriage rendered
them “outsiders” to society’s central institution of love and commitment.
• Religious objections were rejected on the ground that South Africa’s democracy requires
the coexistence of sacred and secular spheres
• Framed marriage equality as integral to post-apartheid constitutional values of dignity,
equality, and freedom.

Value-based constitutionalism
1. Normative
• The Constitution is based on moral values, not just legal rules
2. Interpretive
• Values guide how courts understand and apply rights
3. Transformative
• The Constitution aims to change society and address injustice
4. Contextual
• Must respond to and reflect South Africa’s social and historical context
5. Counter-majoritarian
• Courts can uphold values even against public opinion
